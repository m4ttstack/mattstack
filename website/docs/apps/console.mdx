# console

## What it is

console is the management console for mattstack: a local web app that shows what is running, what needs you and what a run actually did. It reads run state, skill packs and settings through the same client the `rt` CLI and daemon use, so it has no separate database and no write path the CLI does not already have.

## Open it

Open console from the mattstack window. Turn it on or off with [`rt apps`](/rt/reference/apps). It is most useful once rt is set up on your Mac, because without it the board and settings pages start out empty.

## What you do in it

**Watch your runs.** The Runs page shows every pipeline run on this Mac: what waits on you, what is live and what ran earlier, grouped by day. Each row shows the run's pipeline. Filter it by state, or pick a repo in the top bar. When the runs span more than one pipeline, a pipeline picker narrows them to one, in the list and in Timeline, and the page's subtitle names the pipeline you picked. The repo list holds the repos rt has registered. It updates as runs change, with no refreshing. The rail's **Runs** entry counts the decisions waiting on you; click the count to list only those runs. Coming back from a run opens the list as you left it, with the same filter, repo, view and day.

**See a day's runs.** Switch the Runs page to Timeline to see one day's runs as bars across the working day, 8 AM to 6 PM. Switch to Night for the hours from 6 PM to 8 AM. Time a run sat idle or held is not drawn. A calendar beside the bars shades each day by how many runs moved on it; pick a day to open it. Under the calendar, the day's time splits into stage work, waiting on CI and waiting on you. Below that are the decisions you made.

**Follow a live run.** Open a live run for what it is doing now and its story so far. The top bar shows the run's repo and name, and when the page last heard from rt. Its **Runs** button goes back to the list. The header card holds the run's MR and branch. The Now card shows the decisions you already answered in the current stage. The story lists the run's stages beside the one you pick. Each answered decision reads as its record inside its stage: a review's verdict and findings, a reply's threads, or the form with the picks checked. A review or respond run points you to the board for its decisions instead.

**Answer a decision.** When the run asks you a decision, answer it right on the page, one question at a time. A note box under each question carries a note for the agent with your pick. Some questions take an answer in your own words: leave the options alone and write it in the note box, and the run treats it as your instruction. Each question opens with the agent's recommended answer already picked, and a stage's last question, what happens next, opens with **Proceed** when the agent recommends nothing there. Your own words show in the answered decision as `wrote in: "..."`. A decision about evidence boards shows every board it would attach under **Boards to attach**; click one to open it full size and page through the rest.

**Move on to the next decision.** While other runs wait on you, a strip above the run page counts them and links the oldest three. Its **Answer next** button opens the oldest one, and so does the <kbd>g</kbd> key. Its **+N more** link lists the rest on the Runs page.

**Resume a run whose pane is gone.** When a live run has no pane left, its header's menu offers **Resume**, which picks up the run's Claude session in a new pane.

**See what a run did.** A finished run opens as a record. Its header shows how it ended; **Details** opens its numbers and links, with its MR and branch. Its tabs hold its story, its evidence when a work run captured some, and the inputs it ran on. Each decision sits in the story, inside its stage. Search finds a run across every run.

**Check a run's evidence.** The Evidence tab counts the run's cases and shows each one with its before and after screenshots. Under each screenshot is its caption, or the reason it was waived. A waived case carries a gray **waived** badge, and a case captured in both themes carries **light + dark**. Click a screenshot to compare before and after, or see them side by side. The compare view's head counts the cases and steps through them with its arrow buttons or the <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>→</kbd> keys. Its title names the case shown; click it for a list of every case to jump to. It switches between light and dark when the case has both.

**Read a skill pack.** The **Packs** page shows a skill pack in three tabs: **Overview**, **Config** and **Changes**. The **Overview** sidebar lists the pack's **Pipelines**, each with its stages. Under them are its **Commands**, **Board** skills and **Standard** skills. A skill you can run from Claude Code shows its command as you type it there, such as `/acme:work`. Pick a skill and it opens as a tree of blocks, under a trail that leads back up to its pipeline. You can view the whole skill, a block with blocks inside it, or a single block. The skill shows its file first, then a band per section. Its **Other blocks** band gathers the loose ones, and its **Includes** band the trees it includes. Click a row to read its text in place, or **Open** to go into the block. A single block shows its text, with each prop in a box that holds the value each layer passes. A **Layers** strip says how much of the text each layer wrote. Under the text, **Around this block** draws the part of the graph next to it; a block there opens with a click. The **Copy for Claude** button copies a short brief about the block to paste into a session. A block included by other skills lists them under **Used by**, and the skill and each block show what they were **Built from**. A skill not built from blocks, such as a board skill, opens read-only from its headings. console never changes what a prop takes; a pack's own files decide that. When more than one team has a pack, pick the one it reads from the team menu in the top bar. In the mattstack dev app, a dashed **dev** button beside it opens the mattstack plugin's own pack.

**Compile and publish a pack.** The **Config** tab opens on a status bar. It counts the skills that are up to date, need a compile or were never compiled. Beside the counts is the one step that brings Claude the latest. The **Compile Skill Pack** button rebuilds the compiled skills, and the result waits as changes to publish. The **Publish** button shares those changes and installs the pack in Claude Code. The **Install latest** button installs a published version Claude does not have yet. Click a count to list only those skills. Cards under the bar list an org base pack's drift and any stage copy no pipeline needs any more, which a compile removes. They also list any override a pipeline ignores because a later layer dropped that stage. The pack's skills follow, grouped by what they are. Each switch makes a skill public or internal, and the **Apply** button saves the switches you changed. The rail's **Packs** badge counts the skills that need attention and opens **Config** filtered to them.

**Review a pack's changes.** **Changes** lists the pack's commits, with its changes not published yet as the first row. Pick a row to see its diff, file by file. Shift-click a second commit to compare the two: the rows read **From** and **To**, and **Clear compare** ends it. Changes not published yet carry **Discard** and **Publish**, and each asks first, listing every change. On the other tabs, a note says once that a pack has changes to publish. The **Changes** tab counts them. Drag the edge of the commit list or the file list to resize it.

**Follow a block's graph.** A block's **Diagram** opens the **Block map**. **View whole graph** under **Around this block** opens the same map with that block lit, and the trail above it leads back to the block. It shows only blocks: the other steps fold into dotted links, and a small dot marks where a link forks to more than one block. Hover a dotted link to see the steps it holds, and click it to unfold them in place; **Fold** closes them again. Hover a block to light up its links, and click it to open the block. A pill names where a jump goes. A block that jumps more than once gathers its jumps in one chip that lists them. A jump scrolls to its target, rings it, and offers **Back**. The map's **Blocks | Every step** switch shows the whole graph. Each exit's label sits beside the step it leads into.

**Browse and change settings.** The settings explorer lists every registered key across the default, org, team, user and machine scopes, and shows where each value is set. Each section sorts its keys by the scope whose layer sets the value. Keys nothing sets sit last, under **Not set**, and each one's default row says what its default is. Filter by name or by scope; while any setting is broken, a chip counts them and filters to them. A per-project setting picks its project inside its row, starting on the first project that sets it. A single value saves as soon as you commit it: flip a toggle, pick an option, or press Enter or leave the field. A structured value opens as a draft in a JSON editor, with a form one click away, and saves when you press Save. The JSON editor completes and explains each field from its setting's schema. console has no config file of its own, so this is the place to edit settings in the app.

**Look at another team's settings.** On the settings page, the top bar names your org and team. On every page, its right end shows your name and role. An org owner or admin can open any team from the team menu in the top bar, and a team owner the teams they own; a member sees only their own team. While you look at another team, the page lists only its org and team settings, since your user and machine settings are not that team's. A change you make there saves to that team's shared settings. **Back to** your team returns you.

## Settings that matter

console has no keys of its own. What it shows and edits are the shared settings every mattstack app reads; see [Settings](/start/settings).
